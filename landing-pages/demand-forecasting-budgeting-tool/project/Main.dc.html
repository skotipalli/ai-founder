Concept preview — seeking design partners

One trusted number for demand, capacity, and budget.

Today, production planning, procurement, and finance each work from separate, disconnected views of demand, cost, and capacity — and when the budget misses, nobody can tell which factor drove it. This is a concept for one shared forecast-to-budget view across all four roles. It isn't built yet — this page is here to find out if it should be.

Five things are working against today's forecast and budget

Demand

Inaccurate demand forecasts

Downstream production and budget plans inherit the error from day one.

Commodity

Fluctuating raw material prices

Cost estimates go stale faster than the budget cycle that uses them.

Supply risk

Weather and political procurement risk

Supply-side shocks that today's forecasts and budgets don't account for.

Data

Incomplete or inaccurate data

The inputs to forecasting and budgeting are themselves unreliable.

Capacity

Production capacity constraints

Plans that ignore real capacity limits aren't executable plans.

These compound: a forecast built on bad data, blind to raw-material volatility and capacity limits, produces a budget that's wrong in multiple directions at once.

Built around four roles that don't talk to each other today

Production planners

A demand forecast they can plan production runs against, aware of capacity constraints.

Supply chain managers

Visibility into procurement risk — price, weather, political — feeding into the plan.

Procurement teams

Cost estimates that reflect current and projected raw-material price movement.

Finance teams

A budget estimate that ties back to the same forecast and plan, not a separate guess.

Why not just buy a forecasting suite?

Demand forecasting itself is a well-served market — SAP, Kinaxis, o9, and Anaplan all compete on it. What none of them sell is one shared view that ties raw-material price, weather, and political procurement risk directly into a budget number for all four roles above, on top of this company's own data-quality problem. That's the gap: not a forecasting model to build from scratch, but the integration and data-quality layer that connects a bought forecasting engine to a trusted budget.

Directional context from a 2026 market scan: vendors in this space cite roughly 20–50% lower forecast error and 20–30% lower inventory as outer bounds — treat these as something to aim toward, not a promise this concept is making.
